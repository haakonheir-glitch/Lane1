## backward_digits
Backward digit span. Digits 0–9 appear one at a time in the centre of the screen, each for 900 ms with 300 ms blank between them. The person then types the digits in reverse order and presses Enter. Lengths start at 3 with 2 trials per length; the test stops when both trials at a length are wrong, or at length 10. Score: the longest length at which at least one trial was entirely correct (so a person who fails both trials at length 3 scores 0).

## corsi
Corsi block-tapping (forward), on screen. Nine squares are placed irregularly on the screen. Squares light up one at a time, each for 900 ms with 300 ms between them; the person then clicks the squares in the same order. Lengths start at 3 with 2 trials per length; the test stops when both trials at a length are wrong, or at length 10. Score: the longest length at which at least one trial was entirely correct.

## symbol_match
Symbol matching speed test. Each screen shows two sets of three abstract symbols (drawn from 12, such as ◇ △ ○ □ ☆), left and right. The person presses F if both sides contain the same three symbols (order may differ) and J if one symbol differs; the next pair appears immediately. About half the pairs match. After 6 practice pairs, the scored block lasts 90 seconds. Score: number correct minus number incorrect in the 90 seconds.

## choice_rt
Four-choice reaction time. Four squares sit in a row, mapped to the keys D, F, J and K. After a random wait of 600–1800 ms one square lights up, and the person presses its key as fast as possible. After 5 practice trials there are 60 scored trials. Score: median reaction time in milliseconds over correct responses, excluding responses faster than 100 ms. Measured in a browser on a laptop (so it includes keyboard and display latency).
